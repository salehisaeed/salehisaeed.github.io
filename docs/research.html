Research

High-fidelity CFD combined with data-driven methods and machine learning: reinforcement-learning-based flow control, hydraulic turbine transients, reduced-order modelling, physics-informed neural networks and uncertainty quantification.

My research combines computational fluid dynamics (CFD) with data science and machine learning to understand, predict and control complex flows. I aim for methods that are efficient, robust and general enough to be used beyond canonical test cases, and I apply them to problems ranging from canonical flows to engineering systems such as turbomachinery. Most of the methods are developed in OpenFOAM and released as open-source software.

  • Hydraulic turbine transients
  • Modal analysis and ROM

Reinforcement-learning-based flow control

Deep reinforcement learning (DRL) can discover feedback control strategies for nonlinear flows that are hard to design with classical methods. Current DRL–CFD frameworks, however, struggle with efficiency, robustness and generalisation. My work pushes DRL towards realistic flows by combining it with multi-fidelity learning, transfer learning and robust learning under uncertainty.

I developed an intrusive DRL framework in which the agent runs inside the OpenFOAM solver, avoiding external data exchange during training and running parallel environments with MPI. It has been applied to vortex shedding behind 2D and 3D cylinders and to the vortex rope in a swirl generator, a simplified model of a turbine draft tube. The ongoing ÅForsk project extends this to realistic hydraulic turbines.

Key work

  • S. Salehi, Transfer learning strategies for accelerating reinforcement-learning-based flow control, arXiv preprint, 2025
  • S. Salehi and H. Nilsson, A parametric study of axial flow jets for mitigation of vortex rope instabilities, IOP Conf. Ser.: Earth Environ. Sci., 2025

Transient operation of hydraulic turbines

Start-up, shutdown and load changes are becoming frequent as hydropower balances intermittent wind and solar power, and they expose turbines to strong pressure pulsations. Simulating them requires the mesh to follow moving guide vanes and runner blades. I developed a semi-implicit slip algorithm for mesh deformation in complex geometries, implemented in OpenFOAM, and used it for resolved simulations of Francis and Kaplan turbines during transient operation.

These simulations reveal how flow instabilities such as the rotating vortex rope form and decay during transients. The mesh-motion library is released as open source and is used by the R&D department of Vattenfall to study transient operation of their hydropower systems.

Key work

  • S. Salehi, H. Nilsson, E. Lillberg and N. Edh, An in-depth numerical analysis of transient flow field in a Francis turbine during shutdown, Renewable Energy, 2021
  • S. Salehi and H. Nilsson, Flow-induced pulsations in Francis turbines during startup, Renewable Energy, 2022
  • S. Salehi and H. Nilsson, A semi-implicit slip algorithm for mesh deformation in complex geometries, implemented in OpenFOAM, Computer Physics Communications, 2023
  • F. A. Masoodi, S. Salehi and R. Goyal, Reorganization of flow field due to load rejection driven self-mitigation of high load vortex breakdown in a Francis turbine, Physics of Fluids, 2024

Modal analysis and reduced-order modelling

High-fidelity simulations produce large, detailed data sets. Modal decompositions such as proper orthogonal decomposition (POD), spectral POD, dynamic mode decomposition (DMD) and sparsity-promoting DMD extract the coherent structures and dominant frequencies hidden in them. I have used these methods to analyse the vortex rope in hydraulic turbines, identifying the modes that govern the instability. This is a basis for reduced-order models and for designing control strategies.

Key work

  • S. Salehi and H. Nilsson, Modal analysis of vortex rope using dynamic mode decomposition, Physics of Fluids, 2024
  • S. Salehi and H. Nilsson, Dynamic mode decomposition of rotating vortex rope instability, 9th IAHR Meeting of the Work Group on Cavitation and Dynamic Problems in Hydraulic Machinery and Systems, 2024

Physics-informed neural networks

Physics-informed neural networks (PINNs) embed the governing equations in the loss function of a neural network. Together with colleagues at Chalmers University of Technology, I study multi-fidelity PINNs for solving partial differential equations, with applications to the linear wave problem from potential flow theory, comparing soft and hard enforcement of boundary conditions. This work is the PhD project of Mohammad Sheikholeslami, whom I co-supervise.

Key work

  • M. Sheikholeslami, S. Salehi, W. Mao, A. Eslamdoost and H. Nilsson, Physics-informed neural networks with hard and soft boundary conditions for linear free surface waves, Physics of Fluids, 2025
  • M. Sheikholeslami, S. Salehi, W. Mao, A. Eslamdoost and H. Nilsson, State-of-the-art implementations of PINNs for the lid-driven cavity problem: a critical review with future perspectives, Results in Engineering, 2026

Uncertainty quantification

In my doctoral research I developed efficient uncertainty quantification (UQ) methods for turbulent and industrial flows. Sparse polynomial chaos with compressed sensing, and a multi-fidelity ℓ₁-minimisation method, reduce the number of expensive CFD simulations needed while retaining accuracy. Applied to hydraulic pumps, the methods showed that combined operational and geometrical uncertainties significantly affect flow and performance. They were also used for probabilistic CFD and robust optimisation of a cooled gas turbine vane.

  • S. Salehi, M. Raisee, M. J. Cervantes and A. Nourbakhsh, An efficient multifidelity ℓ₁-minimization method for sparse polynomial chaos, Computer Methods in Applied Mechanics and Engineering, 2018

Flexible hydropower: loads, lifetime and energy storage

Together with PhD students at Chalmers University of Technology, I study how flexible operation affects hydropower machines. CFD of a Kaplan turbine model shows how flow-induced forces, pressure pulsations and rotating vortex rope dynamics load the runner at part load and during load reduction, giving input for fatigue lifetime analysis (PhD student Martina Nobilo). In the EU Horizon 2020 project ALPHEUS we studied contra-rotating pump-turbines for low-head pumped hydro storage, including start-up and shutdown sequences and cavitating conditions (PhD student Jonathan Fahlbeck).

Key work

  • M. Nobilo, S. Salehi and H. Nilsson, On the flow-induced pulsating forces during load reduction of a Kaplan turbine model, IOP Conf. Ser.: Earth Environ. Sci., 2025
  • J. Fahlbeck, H. Nilsson, M. H. Arabnejad and S. Salehi, Performance characteristics of a contra-rotating pump-turbine in turbine and pump modes under cavitating flow conditions, Renewable Energy, 2024
